# Resume Less-Is-More Content Audit

## Goal

Make the resume feel sharper, more spacious, and easier to scan without losing the strongest story: Daenon builds trusted AI workflow software, has real operating proof from Ivy Energy, and can mentor non-technical operational teams into practical AI use.

The current content has good raw material. The main issue is repetition: "trusted AI workflows", "real systems", "founder/operator fit", "security", and "grounded context" appear in almost every section. A less-is-more rewrite should let each section own one job.

## Keep

- Positioning around forward-deployed AI product engineering.
- Named product proof: Ludflow, MCPViews, and DecidR MCP.
- Ivy Energy proof: SOC 2 ownership, Snowflake/data movement, integrations, internal tools.
- Mentoring non-technical operators in practical, secure AI use.
- The personal "builder temperament" signal, but only as texture after the proof.

## Cut Or Compress

- Executive summary should not include intro, why-teams-bring-me-in, three metrics, and four value pillars all making the same argument.
- Projects section should not explain the same product thesis in intro, summary, metrics, cards, detail sections, and quick facts.
- Ivy section should avoid saying "operating proof" in the hero, card, details, and quick facts. Show the proof once with concrete outcomes.
- Capabilities should stop restating the whole resume. It should be a compact map of what Daenon can do.
- About should become a short human note. It currently has enough content to feel like a second biography.
- Contact should be almost empty: one sentence, one email route.
- Stale executive `quickFacts` can be removed from the content model because the visual footer was removed.

## Recommended Rewrite Shape

1. Hero:
   - One-line role.
   - Two-sentence proof statement.
   - Three compact proof chips.
   - One short "how I help" paragraph.

2. AI Products:
   - Keep three cards only: Ludflow, MCPViews, DecidR MCP.
   - Each card should answer: what it is, why it matters, what it proves.
   - Remove project detail sections unless we want one expandable "Selected product notes" section later.

3. Ivy Impact:
   - Keep one short intro and one outcomes card.
   - Keep 3 to 4 bullets:
     - Built SOC 2 program.
     - Modernized data movement into Snowflake/downstream workflows.
     - Integrated Salesforce, Zendesk, Slack, ClickUp, Odoo, and related systems.
     - Coached non-technical operators on secure AI-assisted workflows.
   - Remove the second layer of duplicate detail sections unless the page needs long-form depth.

4. Capabilities:
   - Keep four capability cards, but remove most tags.
   - Each card should be one sentence plus 3 to 4 representative tools or behaviors.
   - Avoid repeating project names here unless a capability needs proof.

5. About:
   - Keep one card or one image-backed section.
   - The best version is: curious builder, hardware roots, family in Ogden.
   - Remove duplicate detail sections.

6. Contact:
   - One sentence plus email.
   - Remove quick facts and tags.

## Section-By-Section Notes

### Executive Summary

Current issue: the hero says the same thing through five surfaces: title, intro, "Why Teams Bring Me In", metrics, and value pillars.

Recommendation: keep title, one tight intro, one proof row, and either metrics or value pillars. Not both. My preference is metrics plus one paragraph because the metrics are more scannable.

Suggested direction:

> Forward-deployed AI product engineer building trusted workflow software across product, data, integrations, and security. I work best where founders and operators need someone to find the real problem, shape the product surface, wire the systems, and teach teams how to use AI responsibly.

### AI Products

Current issue: Ludflow, MCPViews, and DecidR are strong proof, but the section over-explains the shared thesis. The user should understand the through-line after reading the three cards.

Recommendation: collapse detail sections into tighter cards. Keep links. Use one sentence of shared thesis at the top.

Card formula:

`[Product] is [plain description]. It proves [specific capability].`

### Ivy Impact

Current issue: the section has excellent substance, but the strongest proof gets diluted by role-fit commentary.

Recommendation: lead with outcomes. Keep the mentoring AI point as a concrete operating outcome, not just a soft skill.

Best proof to preserve:

- SOC 2 program built and maintained.
- Data layer modernization and Snowflake workflows.
- Business-system integrations across Salesforce, Zendesk, Slack, ClickUp, Odoo, and Snowflake.
- Non-technical operational teams coached on secure AI use, output review, sensitive-data handling, and repeatable workflows.

### Capabilities

Current issue: the capabilities section is useful, but the detail sections repeat the hero and Ivy sections.

Recommendation: make this a compact scanning layer. Four cards are good:

- AI workflow product design.
- Data and business-system integration.
- Secure architecture and compliance.
- Product delivery and operator enablement.

The "operator enablement" framing is better than repeating "communication" because it captures mentoring non-technical team members in AI use.

### About

Current issue: this section is warm but long for a resume. It risks competing with the proof sections.

Recommendation: keep only enough to make Daenon memorable: hardware roots, hands-on builder temperament, Ogden/family context. The family photo can stay, but the copy should be compact.

### Contact

Current issue: too much explanation for a simple action.

Recommendation: make it one route:

> For AI product roles, internal platform work, founder-led special projects, or Ludflow/MCP-native conversations, email deej@ludflow.com.

## First Rewrite Pass

- Remove executive `quickFacts`.
- Remove section `quickFacts` from Projects, Ivy, Skills, About, and Contact.
- Remove Projects `detailSections` or reduce them to one short detail section.
- Remove Ivy `detailSections` or reduce to one "Selected outcomes" list.
- Remove Skills `detailSections`; keep cards only.
- Reduce About to one short section.
- Reduce Contact to email only.

This should cut the rendered resume by roughly 35 to 45 percent while making the strongest proof easier to remember.
